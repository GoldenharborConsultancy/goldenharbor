CCRIS / CTOS Record
Late payments hurting your credit score
▾

What's happening

Banks check CCRIS (Central Credit Reference Information System) and CTOS. Late payments, defaults, or too many loans — even settled ones — appear on your record and can cause automatic rejection.

What you can do

① Check your CTOS score (free via CTOS app)
② Settle any outstanding late payments
③ If there are errors, dispute them
④ Wait 6-12 months of clean payment history before reapplying

How we help

We review your CCRIS/CTOS report and tell you exactly what's hurting your score — and which banks are more flexible with credit history.

Wrong or Incomplete Documents
Missing payslips, wrong bank statements
▾

What's happening

Banks are strict about documents. Wrong format payslip, bank statement missing transaction details, EPF not matching declared income — any mismatch triggers rejection.

What you can do

① Ensure payslips show company name, your name, salary breakdown
② Bank statements must be 6 months, show salary credited
③ EPF statement must match your payslip income
④ If self-employed: Form B, SSM, and 6 months bank statements

How we help

We give you a precise checklist for your situation, review every document before you submit, and flag issues early.

Applied to the Wrong Bank
Each bank has different criteria
▾

What's happening

Maybank, CIMB, RHB, Public Bank — they all have different approval criteria. Some favor government staff, some favor high income, some accept lower CTOS scores. Applying to the wrong bank wastes your application and leaves a rejection on your record.

What you can do

① Research each bank's minimum income and credit requirements
② Don't apply to multiple banks at once — each rejection appears on CCRIS
③ Match your profile (income, employment type, credit score) to the right bank

How we help

We know each bank's criteria. We match your profile to the bank most likely to approve you — saving rejections on your record.

Income vs. Commitment (DSR)
Your debt service ratio is too high
▾

What's happening

Banks calculate DSR (Debt Service Ratio) = total monthly commitments ÷ monthly income. If you have car loan + credit card + PTPTN + existing personal loan, your DSR might exceed the bank's limit (usually 60-70%).

What you can do

① Calculate your DSR: add all monthly commitments ÷ net income
② Pay down credit card balances and smaller loans first
③ Consider a longer tenure to lower the monthly commitment shown
④ Consolidate multiple debts into one lower monthly payment

How we help

We calculate your DSR, identify which commitments are hurting you, and advise on the right loan amount and tenure that fits your profile.
