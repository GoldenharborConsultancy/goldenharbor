Understanding CCRIS After a Loan Rejection

Updated: 15 October 2026 · 6 min read

A loan rejection does not mean CCRIS has blacklisted you. CCRIS is a credit information system, not an approval or blacklist service. Banks consider its information alongside other application details.

Ask the bank what it can clarify about the decision, then review your credit reports, affordability and documents before applying again. No adviser can promise the next application will be approved.

Why Might a Bank Reject a Loan Application?

There is no single reason that applies to every applicant. Possible factors to review include:

  • Repayment history — reported arrears or other credit concerns
  • Affordability — income and existing commitments relative to the proposed repayment
  • Income evidence and documents — missing, unclear or inconsistent information; use our personal and SME loan document checklist as a preparation guide
  • Product requirements — the bank's current eligibility and lending criteria

The report alone cannot establish the reason for rejection. Bank Negara Malaysia's CCRIS FAQ explains that banks make their own lending decisions and CCRIS is not a blacklist.

What is CCRIS?

CCRIS is the Central Credit Reference Information System, managed by Bank Negara Malaysia. It contains credit information supplied by participating financial institutions, including:

  • Loan accounts — housing, car, personal, business
  • Credit cards — limits, balances, payment history
  • Payment patterns — reported repayment status and months in arrears

Banks may use CCRIS information alongside income, commitments and their own lending criteria. CCRIS does not make approval decisions.

Step-by-Step: Review Your CCRIS Report

Step 1: Get Your CCRIS Report

Access your own CCRIS report through Bank Negara Malaysia's official eCCRIS portal. Follow its current registration and identity verification instructions; do not share your login credentials with an adviser.

💡 Tip: CCRIS and CTOS reports are different products. Our CTOS report guide explains the distinction and which report options include a score.

Step 2: Check Accounts and Possible Errors

Review accounts with reported arrears and check whether the information is accurate. A report alone does not identify the bank's exact reason for rejection. Items to review include:

  • An unfamiliar account or balance that needs clarification
  • Reported late payments on a car loan or credit card
  • A PTPTN loan in arrears
  • Existing loan repayments that need to be included in your affordability review

Step 3: Discuss Arrears With Your Lender

If you have overdue payments, contact the lender to confirm the amount owed and discuss repayment options. Keep proof of any agreed arrangement and payments.

⚠️ Important: A repayment does not erase accurate payment history. CCRIS includes a 12-month repayment history; other information and updates depend on the record type and reporting institution. Query possible errors with that institution.

Step 4: Review Affordability and DSR

Your debt service ratio (DSR) compares monthly debt repayments with income. Banks may use different income definitions, calculations and limits. Eligibility also does not necessarily mean a repayment is affordable for you. Review:

  • Income evidence — the income the bank accepts for assessment
  • Existing commitments — current repayments and other obligations
  • Proposed repayment — affordability under the bank's criteria

Discuss affordability concerns with your lender or a qualified debt adviser before changing repayment arrangements or taking on additional borrowing.

Step 5: Review Before Reapplying

Review any pending applications and the bank's feedback before applying again. Avoid assuming that another bank will make the same decision.

Reapplication timing depends on your circumstances and the bank's current criteria; there is no universal waiting period.

Common Questions

Can you remove accurate CCRIS records?

No. We cannot delete accurate credit records. If you believe information is inaccurate, contact the reporting financial institution to request an investigation and correction where appropriate.

⚠️ Important: Be cautious of promises to delete accurate credit history for a fee. Resolving arrears and disputing reporting errors are different from removing accurate records.

Can I apply for a loan if my CCRIS report shows arrears?

Eligibility and pricing depend on the bank, product and your full circumstances. We cannot predict approval or the rate a bank may offer.

How long does repayment history appear in CCRIS?

The report includes a 12-month repayment history. Do not assume every type of credit information disappears after that period; ask the reporting institution about the relevant record and update process.
